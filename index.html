01DEEP TIME

BEFORE THE NATIONS

The cradle.

Long before modern borders, the landscapes of present-day Kenya were home to ancient human ancestors. The fossil-rich sites around Lake Turkana have helped researchers understand human evolution.

01

Koobi Fora
On the east shore of Lake Turkana, its sediments span about five to one million years. According to the National Museums of Kenya, it produced a Homo erectus skull about 1.6 million years old and, by 1994, about 200 hominid specimens. It is part of Lake Turkana National Parks, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [1]

The Turkana Boy skeleton, found at Nariokotome on the west shore in 1984, is the best-known find in the region.

03COASTAL HISTORY

THE INDIAN OCEAN WORLD

The coast
looks outward.

Kenya's coast became part of a wider Indian Ocean world. Coastal towns developed through trade, migration, cultural exchange and local histories. Swahili culture grew from these long interactions.

03

Fort Jesus
The Portuguese built the fort in Mombasa from 1593, on a coral ridge at the harbour entrance, to a design by the Italian engineer Batista Cairato. Omani Arabs attacked it from 1696 to 1698. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 2011. [2]

04LATE 19TH–20TH CENTURY

COLONIAL OCCUPATION

The railway
and the colony.

British colonial rule transformed land ownership, labour, political authority and everyday life. A railway connected the coast to the interior while serving colonial economic and administrative interests.

04

The protectorate
Britain proclaimed its protectorate on 1 July 1895. Fort Jesus in Mombasa was converted into a prison. [2]

The railway reached Kisumu in 1901, Nairobi began as a railway depot, and Kenya became a Crown Colony in 1920.

05RESISTANCE AND INDEPENDENCE

THE FIGHT FOR FREEDOM

The struggle
for freedom.

Resistance to colonial rule took many forms across the country. The Mau Mau movement, which began among the Kikuyu, became a major part of the struggle that led to independence in 1963. [3]

05

The Emergency
The movement was banned in 1950. In October 1952 the colonial government declared a state of emergency. Britannica reports more than 11,000 rebels killed by the end of 1956, about 100 Europeans and 2,000 African loyalists, and more than 20,000 Kikuyu held in detention camps. [3]

06

The 2013 settlement
On 6 June 2013 the British Government announced an out-of-court settlement: a statement of regret, damages of KSh 330,000 each to 5,228 claimants, and a contribution to a monument for victims of colonial-era torture. [4]

Other resistance leaders include Koitalel arap Samoei (killed in 1905) and Mekatilili wa Menza, who led the Giriama in 1913–14.

A COUNTRY STILL BECOMING

A republic
of 47.

The 2010 Constitution declares Kenya a sovereign, multi-party republic divided into 47 counties, with national and county governments that are distinct and interdependent. [5]

07

What the Constitution says
Kiswahili is the national language, and Kiswahili and English are the official languages. There is no state religion. Article 11 recognises culture as the foundation of the nation. Madaraka Day (1 June), Mashujaa Day (20 October) and Jamhuri Day (12 December) are national days. [5]

Wangari Maathai won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004, and M-Pesa launched in 2007.
