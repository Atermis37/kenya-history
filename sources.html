RESEARCH AND DOCUMENTATION

The reference
desk.

History deserves more than attractive design. This page records the sources used, the claims that still need checking and the research gaps that should be addressed before publication.

01 / SOURCES USED

Starting references.

Each numbered footnote on the site points to one of these. They are starting points and should be read alongside academic research and community perspectives.

  1. SOURCE 01 / HUMAN ORIGINS

    National Museums of Kenya — Koobi Fora

    Used for the sediment sequence of about five to one million years, the Homo erectus skull of about 1.6 million years, the roughly 200 hominid specimens recovered by 1994, and the World Heritage status of Lake Turkana National Parks.

    Visit National Museums of Kenya ↗
  2. SOURCE 02 / COASTAL HISTORY AND COLONIAL RULE

    National Museums of Kenya — Fort Jesus Museum

    Used for the Portuguese construction from 1593, the design by Batista Cairato, the Omani attack of 1696–1698, the UNESCO listing in 2011, the proclamation of the protectorate on 1 July 1895, and the fort's use as a prison.

    Visit National Museums of Kenya ↗
  3. SOURCE 03 / RESISTANCE AND INDEPENDENCE

    Encyclopaedia Britannica — Mau Mau

    Used for the origin of the movement among the Kikuyu, the 1950 ban, the October 1952 state of emergency, the casualty and detention figures to the end of 1956, and independence in 1963. This is a single reference work, and the figures should be checked against Caroline Elkins' Imperial Reckoning.

    Read the Britannica entry ↗
  4. SOURCE 04 / HISTORICAL JUSTICE

    Kenya Human Rights Commission — The Mau Mau Settlement: Setting the Record Straight

    Published 21 June 2013. Used for the 6 June 2013 settlement: the statement of regret, damages of KSh 330,000 each to 5,228 claimants, and the contribution to a monument.

    Read the Kenya Human Rights Commission statement ↗
  5. SOURCE 05 / THE REPUBLIC

    The Constitution of Kenya, 2010

    Published by the National Council for Law Reporting (Kenya Law). Used for the 47 counties (First Schedule), Kiswahili as the national language and Kiswahili and English as official languages (Article 7), no state religion (Article 8), the national days (Article 9) and culture as the foundation of the nation (Article 11). The link is a copy hosted by the National Museums of Kenya.

    Open the Constitution ↗
  6. SOURCE 06 / HUMAN ORIGINS

    Smithsonian Institution Human Origins Program — KNM-WT 15000 (Turkana Boy)

    Used for the Nariokotome site, the 1984 discovery by Kamoya Kimeu, the age of about 1.6 million years, Homo erectus, the 40% completeness, the age at death of eight or nine, and the height of about 1.6 m.

    Visit the Smithsonian page ↗
  7. SOURCE 07 / PEOPLE

    The Nobel Foundation — Wangari Maathai, Biographical

    Used for the 1976 start of the tree-planting idea, the Green Belt Movement and the 2004 Nobel Peace Prize.

    Read the Nobel biography ↗
  8. SOURCE 08 / MOBILE MONEY

    Safaricom Newsroom — The M-PESA wonder: how it all begun (2017)

    Used for the 6 March 2007 launch and the customer figure of more than 25 million in 2017. This is the operator's own account, so its growth claims are company statements and should be cross-checked independently.

    Read the Safaricom article ↗
  9. SOURCE 09 / SWAHILI COAST

    UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Historic Town and Archaeological Site of Gedi

    Used for Gedi's importance from the 10th to the 17th centuries, its Indian Ocean trade links, the imported goods found there, its exports, and its position 6.5 km inland.

    Visit UNESCO ↗
  10. SOURCE 10 / SWAHILI COAST

    UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Lamu Old Town

    Used for Lamu being the oldest and best-preserved Swahili settlement in East Africa and having been inhabited continuously for over 700 years.

    Visit UNESCO ↗
  11. SOURCE 11 / THE RAILWAY

    Kenya Railways Corporation — History

    Used for the construction start on 30 May 1896, the railhead reaching Nairobi in 1899, completion at Kisumu on 19 December 1901, and the figure of about 2,500 deaths. This is a state operator's account and is celebratory in tone. Only dates and figures are used, and the death toll is the company's estimate.

    Visit Kenya Railways ↗
  12. SOURCE 12 / RESISTANCE

    University of Sheffield, History Matters — Mekatilili Wa Menza and the Giriama War (2021)

    Used for Mekatilili's birth decade, her activity in the early 1910s, the kaya fungo meetings and oaths, her arrest on 17 October 1913, the Giriama regaining the kaya in 1919, and her death in 1924. A university blog by a historian that cites Paterson (1970), Brantley (1981) and Temu (1971). It is not peer reviewed, and some of its dates are inconsistent, so only the safer points are used.

    Read the article ↗
  13. SOURCE 13 / INDEPENDENCE

    Encyclopaedia Britannica — Kenya: World War II to independence

    Used for the state of emergency from October 1952 to 1960, Kenyatta's imprisonment, the 1963 elections and independence on 12 December 1963, and the republic a year later.

    HISTORY.com on 12 December 1963 ↗ Read the Britannica article ↗
  14. SOURCE 14 / RESISTANCE

    Koitalel arap Samoei: Al Jazeera, Nation Africa and The EastAfrican

    Used for his role as orkoiyot of the Nandi, his killing on 19 October 1905 by Colonel Richard Meinertzhagen at a meeting he had been invited to as a truce, and Meinertzhagen's admission in his memoir Kenya Diary. Accounts differ on details such as how many others died and who touched whom, so only the core facts are used. The story that his head was taken to Britain is oral history and is not used.

    Read the Al Jazeera feature ↗ Nation Africa ↗ The EastAfrican ↗
  15. SOURCE 15 / COLONIAL RULE

    Kenya Gazette, 23 July 1920, and UK Parliament Hansard, 12 July 1920

    Primary records of the Order in Council that annexed the East Africa Protectorate as the Colony of Kenya, a Crown colony without responsible government.

    Read the Gazette ↗ Read Hansard ↗
  16. SOURCE 16 / MIGRATIONS

    Britannica, the National Museums of Kenya, a US Library of Congress country study and the Journal of African History

    Used for the three language families, Cushitic herders in the arid north and east, the Kalenjin expansion around the middle of the last millennium, Bantu speakers in the Lake Victoria region by about the eleventh century, and oral histories of Kikuyu and Kamba arrival. The Journal of African History study re-examines the Shungwaya account, and sources date the Nilotic arrival differently, so every date is approximate.

    Britannica ↗ National Museums of Kenya ↗ Library of Congress study ↗ Journal of African History ↗
  17. SOURCE 17 / SWAHILI COAST

    OpenStax World History and the World History Encyclopedia

    Used for coastal trade with Arabia, Persia, India and China, the role of Islam in Swahili identity, and the meaning of Swahili as people of the coast.

    OpenStax ↗ World History Encyclopedia ↗
  18. SOURCE 18 / LANGUAGE

    Mous and Barasa on Sheng, and IFRA Nairobi

    Used for Sheng's origins among Nairobi youth in the 1960s and 1970s, its Swahili base with English and other Kenyan languages, its spread through music, and its use beyond youth and Nairobi. Sources date its origins differently, from the early 1950s to the 1970s.

    Mous and Barasa ↗ IFRA Nairobi ↗
  19. SOURCE 19 / MUSIC

    Music In Africa, the Nation and Nairobi News

    Used for benga's emergence from Luo rhythms with Congolese influences in the 1970s, the 1950s and 1960s pioneers, Malaika by Fadhili William, and the newer genge, kapuka and gengetone styles.

    Music In Africa ↗ Nation ↗ Nairobi News ↗

02 / RESEARCH GAPS

Still to verify.

These claims appear on the site marked "source pending" and should not be treated as verified.

Historical details requiring further research

  • The wider effects of colonial rule on land, labour and political authority (Jedwab's paper has not yet been read).
  • The Kenya Railways death toll of about 2,500 is the company's own figure and has not been cross-checked.
  • Mekatilili's exact arrest and release dates should be checked against Paterson (1970) and Brantley (1981), because the blog's chronology is inconsistent.
  • Koitalel's start year as orkoiyot differs between sources (1890 or 1895) and is not stated on the site.
  • Migration dates are approximate and disputed. The journals.co.za papers and the BBC, Knowable and Lumen pages supplied for this topic have not yet been read.
  • Film, the Agikuyu and the remaining community details: sources supplied but not yet read.
  • M-Pesa's figures come from Safaricom itself and need independent confirmation.
  • Whether Harambee appears on the coat of arms, and the food and running entries in the culture section.

03 / COMMUNITY RESEARCH

More than a short profile.

The community profiles are introductory drafts. They do not represent every sub-group, local tradition, language or historical experience.

Community review needed

  • All 12 community entries need further research and review. Specific claims are marked "source pending" in each profile.
  • Where possible, members of each community and knowledgeable cultural contributors should review the descriptions.
  • Population figures should come from the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics 2019 Census, Volume IV. The report series page has been identified, but the volume itself has not yet been read.
  • On the culture section, the official languages, Article 11, Sheng, music and M-Pesa entries are sourced. Harambee, food and running are pending.

05 / EDITORIAL STANDARD

Accuracy before appearance.

Our research rule

Uncertainty must remain visible. A visually attractive website is not a reason to publish unverified historical claims. Dates, names, population figures and cultural descriptions should be checked before they are presented as established facts.
