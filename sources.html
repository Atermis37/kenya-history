RESEARCH AND DOCUMENTATION

The reference
desk.

History deserves more than attractive design. This page records the sources used, the claims that still need checking and the research gaps that should be addressed before publication.

01 / SOURCES USED

Starting references.

Each numbered footnote on the site points to one of these. They are starting points and should be read alongside academic research and community perspectives.

  1. SOURCE 01 / HUMAN ORIGINS

    National Museums of Kenya — Koobi Fora

    Used for the sediment sequence of about five to one million years, the Homo erectus skull of about 1.6 million years, the roughly 200 hominid specimens recovered by 1994, and the World Heritage status of Lake Turkana National Parks.

    Visit National Museums of Kenya ↗
  2. SOURCE 02 / COASTAL HISTORY AND COLONIAL RULE

    National Museums of Kenya — Fort Jesus Museum

    Used for the Portuguese construction from 1593, the design by Batista Cairato, the Omani attack of 1696–1698, the UNESCO listing in 2011, the proclamation of the protectorate on 1 July 1895, and the fort's use as a prison.

    Visit National Museums of Kenya ↗
  3. SOURCE 03 / RESISTANCE AND INDEPENDENCE

    Encyclopaedia Britannica — Mau Mau

    Used for the origin of the movement among the Kikuyu, the 1950 ban, the October 1952 state of emergency, the casualty and detention figures to the end of 1956, and independence in 1963. This is a single reference work, and the figures should be checked against Caroline Elkins' Imperial Reckoning.

    Read the Britannica entry ↗
  4. SOURCE 04 / HISTORICAL JUSTICE

    Kenya Human Rights Commission — The Mau Mau Settlement: Setting the Record Straight

    Published 21 June 2013. Used for the 6 June 2013 settlement: the statement of regret, damages of KSh 330,000 each to 5,228 claimants, and the contribution to a monument.

    Read the Kenya Human Rights Commission statement ↗
  5. SOURCE 05 / THE REPUBLIC

    The Constitution of Kenya, 2010

    Published by the National Council for Law Reporting (Kenya Law). Used for the 47 counties (First Schedule), Kiswahili as the national language and Kiswahili and English as official languages (Article 7), no state religion (Article 8), the national days (Article 9) and culture as the foundation of the nation (Article 11). The link is a copy hosted by the National Museums of Kenya.

    Open the Constitution ↗

02 / RESEARCH GAPS

Still to verify.

These claims appear on the site marked "source pending" and should not be treated as verified.

Historical details requiring further research

  • Turkana Boy: the Nariokotome discovery in 1984, including the discovery details and the people involved.
  • Cushitic, Nilotic and Bantu migration accounts, including the chronology and regional differences.
  • Swahili city-state trade, including the histories of Gedi and Lamu.
  • The railway route, its completion at Kisumu in 1901, Nairobi's development and the 1920 Crown Colony date.
  • Koitalel arap Samoei and the events surrounding his death in 1905.
  • Mekatilili wa Menza and the Giriama resistance of 1913–1914.
  • Wangari Maathai's 2004 Nobel Peace Prize and the history of M-Pesa from 2007.
  • The month and day of independence in 1963 (Britannica's Mau Mau entry does not give them).

03 / COMMUNITY RESEARCH

More than a short profile.

The community profiles are introductory drafts. They do not represent every sub-group, local tradition, language or historical experience.

Community review needed

  • All 12 community entries need further research and review. Specific claims are marked "source pending" in each profile.
  • Where possible, members of each community and knowledgeable cultural contributors should review the descriptions.
  • Population figures should come from the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics 2019 Census, Volume IV. The report series page has been identified, but the volume itself has not yet been read.
  • On the culture section, only the official-languages and Article 11 entries are verified. The rest are pending.

05 / EDITORIAL STANDARD

Accuracy before appearance.

Our research rule

Uncertainty must remain visible. A visually attractive website is not a reason to publish unverified historical claims. Dates, names, population figures and cultural descriptions should be checked before they are presented as established facts.
